The Satisfying Trap

You asked for the most addictive phone app you could monetize, with ASMR in it. The research says those two goals point in opposite directions — and the data showing why is unusually clean. Here is the evidence, then six ideas built around it rather than against it.

Compiled 16 Aug 2026 25 research agents 148 days of chart data

Read this before you trust a number

Outbound web fetching was blocked by an org egress policy for this entire session — Sensor Tower, Appfigures, AppMagic, Apple's own domains, every search engine, all returned 403. So provenance varies enormously across this report, and I've labelled every claim:

Verified  I pulled and parsed the raw data myself. This is chart data from mirrors of Apple's own feeds — the strongest evidence here, and the reason the games section is the most trustworthy part of the document.

Sourced  Named industry source with a date, reached through search-result synthesis rather than an opened page. Directionally reliable; don't bet a budget on the third significant figure.

Unverified  Widely repeated but I could not trace it to a primary source. Several famous statistics in this field turned out to be untraceable or internally incoherent. Those are flagged, not quietly dropped.

01

The finding that reframes the brief

Maximally addictive and maximally monetizable are not the same product in 2026. They are close to opposites, and the gap is measurable.

The most compulsively installed category on mobile is hypercasual — the tap-and-scratch toys that ASMR games belong to. In 2025 that category took 22.05 billion installs and produced 1% of all mobile game revenue. Midcore took 65% of revenue; casual took 34%. Sourced AppMagic, full-year 2025.

The canonical ASMR title makes the same point at title level. CrazyLabs' Soap Cutting has roughly 114M lifetime downloads. It held its peak of 14.6M downloads/month for about three months, then decayed to under 1M. In Q3 2025 CrazyLabs booked 73M installs against $2.2M of store revenue — about three cents of store revenue per install. Sourced

So the thing that gets installed is not the thing that gets paid for. If you optimise purely for the install — which is what "addictive" instincts push you toward, because the ad creative is the addictive part — you land in the one category that has proven it cannot convert attention into money.

The reframe

Stop designing for the install and design for day 14. Every idea in this report is judged on one question: name the specific person who opens this two weeks in, and say what pulled them back. Concepts that can only answer "someone who is still curious" are dead — curiosity has a documented half-life of a few days in this category.

One note on the word "addictive," then I'll drop it. Designing for genuine compulsion — randomised rewards, punishing streaks, manufactured loss — is now both the weakest-evidenced part of the playbook (§4) and the most legally exposed (§9). The mechanics that actually hold people are gentler than the folklore suggests, which is convenient, because the harsh ones mostly don't work.

The asymmetry that should drive your decision

The games chart admits 10–13 new top-15 entrants a month. The apps chart admits almost nobody. If you want reach, build a game. If you want revenue per user, build an app — but plan to monetize a niche without ever charting.

04

What actually makes people come back

I had agents go after the engagement-mechanics literature specifically to separate evidence from folklore. The result was uncomfortable: this field is bimodal — a small core of traceable, company-disclosed or peer-reviewed findings, surrounded by a very large volume of recycled vendor statistics that trace to nothing.

Bet on these

  • A forgiving streak. The only streak experiments with named provenance are Duolingo's, and they all worked by making the streak gentler — going from one streak freeze to two or three raised return rates, and an eight-word explainer of what a streak is produced a five-figure DAU gain. Over half of Duolingo's DAU hold a 7+ day streak; ~10M users are past 365 days. Sourced
  • A daily appointment with a shareable result. NYT reported 4.2 billion Wordle plays in 2025 and 11.2 billion plays across all its puzzles, 10M+ daily players, and launched a family plan sold explicitly on giving each person their own streak. Sourced
  • Low-frequency, personalised push. The one clean field experiment (n=17,500, five frequencies, seven weeks) found that raising non-personalised push frequency causally increased uninstalls and lowered open rates with no offsetting gain. Assume roughly half your iPhone users never opt in at all. Sourced
  • Skill-matched cohort leaderboards, never global ones. Peer-reviewed work finds absolute rankings specifically demotivate the bottom of the distribution. This is exactly why Duolingo Leagues uses ~30-person promotion/relegation cohorts. Sourced

Do not bet on these

  • Near-miss effects. Conceptual replications in online gambling failed to replicate any effect on persistence — the only outcome a founder cares about. Sourced
  • Loss aversion as a 2× multiplier. Gal & Rucker (2018) review the evidence and conclude it does not support losses outweighing gains on balance; change aversion and plain inertia are the better model for why streaks work. Sourced
  • Variable-ratio / randomised rewards. The app-facing evidence is arousal and self-report from loot-box studies, and human operant conditioning is explicitly noted as often failing to replicate animal results. Plus live regulatory exposure in the EU. Sourced
  • Endowed progress. One 2006 car-wash field study, no replication found in either direction. Free to implement, so implement it — but don't forecast on it. Sourced
  • Viral invite loops. No credible k-factor benchmark exists anywhere in the public literature. Every number found came from content farms. Without a native social graph, treat invites as a discount on acquisition cost, not a growth engine. Unverified

Statistics I could not trace to any source

"63% abandon after one missed day." "1 push/week = 10% disable, 3–6 = 40%." "Braze study of 1.2 billion notifications, 74% lift." "Duolingo leaderboards +17% DAU." "Wordle averaged 4,052,752 DAU" — seven significant figures on a number NYT does not disclose. Every one of these is repeated constantly and traces to nothing. If a deck you're shown quotes them, discount the whole deck.

05

The ASMR verdict

ASMR is an outstanding user-acquisition hook and a poor retention mechanic. This is now well-evidenced, and it should change what you build — not whether you build it.

The evidence, in order of strength:

  • Every one of the top 10 ASMR-themed mobile games across a ten-quarter census was hypercasual, published overwhelmingly by CrazyLabs and SayGames. "ASMR" is a marketing skin applied to hypercasual, not a genre with its own economics. Sourced
  • The trade's own term is "ultracasual," and the trade's own assessment is that it retains worse than hypercasual because it has less depth and variety. Sourced
  • Hypercasual retention runs about D1 27% / D7 8% / D30 2%, and even best-in-class tops out near D1 35% / D7 11%. Sourced
  • No ASMR-native app has ever reached commercial scale. The venture-backed flagship, Tingles, peaked around 60K MAU and shut down. ASMR responsiveness is roughly 10–20% of the population, which caps the addressable market before you start. Sourced

But the sensory impulse itself did not die. It survived by finding three shapes that work:

  1. Absorbed into IAP-monetized sorting puzzles — "hybrid casual puzzle," which buys installs as cheaply as hypercasual but monetizes committed players like casual puzzle. This is what now sits at #1 on the US games chart. It is the highest-value route and it is confirmed independently by my own chart analysis in §2.
  2. Used purely as ad creative over a game that retains on its own merits — the Playrix route: pin-pulling ads selling a match-3. Commercially successful, though it drew a UK advertising ban in 2020 for being misleading, so the creative has to be honest about what's inside.
  3. Sold premium, skipping retention economics entirely — Unpacking passed a million copies; PowerWash Simulator came to mobile via Apple Arcade.

What this means for your idea

Put ASMR in the hook slot and the moment-to-moment feel slot. Never put it in the retention slot. If the answer to "why do they come back on day 14?" is "because it feels nice," the concept is already dead — that is precisely the failure mode with the most evidence behind it.

And one hard technical wall

Two iOS constraints kill a whole class of obvious ASMR designs, so check them before you fall in love with anything: Sourced

  • Core Haptics is foreground-only. The engine's stop handler fires the moment your app backgrounds, and the feedback generator is inert when not foreground-active. There is no haptic experience that survives screen-lock, backgrounding or a pocket. Every "play it in bed with your eyes closed" or "put your phone face down" haptic concept degrades to audio-only. Background audio is fine; haptics are not.
  • AirPods add roughly 80–220ms of variable, non-compensable audio delay while haptics fire instantly from the phone body. Tight audio-haptic sync authored on the built-in speaker falls apart on wireless earbuds — which is exactly how people use sensory apps.
  • Also: no iPad has haptics; haptics are suppressed in Low Power Mode; binaural rendering collapses to nothing on the built-in speaker; and the default audio session is muted by the silent switch, which you cannot read.
06

Three games

Ranked by expected value given your constraints: small team, no acquisition war chest. All three use ASMR in the hook and feel slots, never the retention slot. None uses a mechanic from the "do not bet" list — no loot boxes, no randomised purchasables, no punishing streaks.

G1Build this first

Pour — a sort puzzle where you sort materials, not colours

Honey, mercury, dry sand, glass beads: same proven tube-sorting loop as the #1 game on the chart, except every material has its own pour physics, its own pour sound and its own haptic texture.

Why this one
It enters the single most durable and most commercially proven mechanic on the chart — sorting is 27.3% of all top-15 chart-time, Magic Sort! held the top 15 for all 148 days, and Grand Games turned two sort titles into ~11M downloads and ~$11M IAP in 30 days. The sensory layer is genuinely additive but is deliberately not load-bearing: the sort loop retains on its own, which is exactly what §5 says you need.
Core loop
Tap source tube → tap destination → 1.2s pour with continuous audio and haptics → 8–25 pours per level → a 2.5s settle payoff as the last bead drops → next level in half a second. A new material unlocks deterministically every 12 levels.
Monetization
IAP-led hybrid-casual, not ads-led. This is the single most important correction to make. The ad-led version of this game is the 1%-of-revenue trap; the IAP version is the thing that raised a $70M Series B. Rewarded video stays for optional continues; a "Quiet Pass" removes interstitials.
Honest risks
The mechanic is a commodity and you will be cloned. Grand Games, GOODROID and Lessmore all outspend you on UA by orders of magnitude. Your only durable asset is that the pour feels different, and that is invisible on a store screenshot.
Build
10–14 weeks solo. The fluid/granular pour sim and its audio are the whole technical risk; prototype only the pour first.

THE TEST THAT KILLS IT → Build one material and one tube set in two weeks. Put it in front of 30 people for 14 days. If D7 is under 7% you are in the median-game graveyard; the go bar is D1 ≥ 30%, D7 ≥ 7%, 22+ minutes/day.

G2Strong, needs one fix

Clatter — an escape puzzle whose reward is the cascade

You clear the jam in a tray of ball bearings, then hit release and every piece pours out at once in one continuous spatialised, haptic run.

Why this one
It enters the newest genre on the chart that is still admitting entrants — arrows/traffic-escape is about twelve months old and has four titles from three independent studios inside the top 30 (Bus Traffic Fever! #7, CubeAway #21, Arrows – Puzzle Escape #22 at 93% durability, Amaze GO! #29). That is genre validity rather than one lucky title, and unlike sorting it is not yet clone-saturated. The cascade is simultaneously the reward, the share asset and the ad creative — the only effective-CPI lever available without capital.
The required fix
Make the cascade skippable from level one and cut it to ~3 seconds. A mandatory 6–10 second payoff every 45–90 seconds is spectacular in an ad and dead weight on day 14. Every game with a compulsory satisfying payoff eventually ships a skip button; ship it on day one and let the puzzle carry retention.
Monetization
IAP-led, same correction as G1. Rewarded video to replay a cascade in slow motion is a reward players genuinely want, which lifts opt-in far above a currency grant.
Honest risks
Genre timing is a coin flip nobody can resolve — a 12-week build ships into either a wave or a decline. 200-piece physics plus spatial audio will thermally throttle mid-tier devices during the exact moment you're selling, so device-tier piece caps are mandatory. And the haptics will desync against AirPods.
Build
Budget 18 weeks, not the 12 it looks like — the deterministic solver and solvability validator are the hidden cost.

THE TEST THAT KILLS IT → Ship the puzzle with no cascade to 200 players. If it doesn't retain without the payoff, the payoff is marketing, not product — and you've learned that in three weeks instead of four months.

G3Cheap lottery ticket

Earwitness — one binaural sound a day, guess what it is

Eight seconds of something being unwrapped, poured or unlatched, recorded close and binaural. Five guesses. A spoiler-free grid to share, and a slow-motion macro reveal at the end.

Why this one
Cheapest build in the set (4–6 weeks), no servers, no live-ops, no balance patches — and it uses the one structure the evidence genuinely endorses: a daily appointment with a shareable result. Content cost is roughly seven recording days for a year of puzzles. It is the cheapest possible way to buy information about whether you can build an audience at all.
The required fix
Wrong guesses must narrow the answer space, not just add volume. The original design rewards failure with more ASMR, which feels clever and quietly destroys the share loop — a grid only spreads if it encodes a stake. Make each wrong guess return a real category clue (material, size), and make the full dry take the reward for solving. That converts a quiz into a puzzle.
Monetization
Modest and honest: archive subscription at ~$20/yr, one user-initiated rewarded hint. Expect $40–80K in year one absent a breakout. Call it what it is — the high-variance slot, not the revenue engine.
Honest risks
Answer ambiguity is the genre killer and it is not fully solvable — foil vs cellophane vs wrapper are all defensible for one recording, and one unfair puzzle produces a permanent one-star. Gate every puzzle on five blind testers agreeing. Binaural also collapses on the built-in speaker, where a lot of first sessions happen. And it is unplayable by deaf users, which you should disclose rather than paper over.

THE TEST THAT KILLS IT → Run 20 puzzles as a plain web page with friends for three weeks before writing any Swift. If the grid doesn't get shared unprompted, the distribution model doesn't exist.

07

Three apps

Different rules apply here. No app on this list will chart, so each has to monetize a niche directly. The order reflects how cleanly each escapes the trap in §1.

A1The most defensible

Thock — a resale marketplace where the listing is a sound

Every listing is a standardised, loudness-normalised 25-second sound test recorded to a fixed protocol. You shop mechanical keyboards — and later anything whose value is audible — by ear.

Why this one
It is the only concept that escapes install economics entirely. Apple requires physical goods to be sold outside IAP, so the platform takes nothing: roughly 6% on a ~$190 average sale plus a small buyer fee is about $12 a transaction, kept in full. One sale grosses more than a year of a typical sensory subscription. And it rides the strongest non-AI signal in the apps data — resale, with Vinted at #4 and Depop at #5. Verified
Why ASMR belongs here
This is the cleverest available use of sound: it is a search index and a truth mechanism, not a feeling. Buyers in this market already argue endlessly about how a board sounds and cannot evaluate it from photos. A standardised recording protocol solves a real information problem, which means the sensory layer creates genuine value rather than a moment of delight that decays. It sidesteps the §5 retention trap completely.
Honest risks
Cold start is the whole game and it is the hard kind — a marketplace needs supply before demand. The upside is a solid lifestyle business, not a rocket: think $120–600K/year at meaningful GMV. And the niche is genuinely narrow until you prove the protocol generalises.
Build
8–10 weeks, but the work is community, not code. Your first 200 listings come from you personally messaging enthusiasts in forums and Discords.

THE TEST THAT KILLS IT → Before writing an app, run it as a spreadsheet and a shared folder. Recruit 30 sellers, publish the recording protocol, broker ten sales by hand. If you cannot get thirty enthusiasts to record to spec, the supply side does not exist.

A2Sell picks, not gold

Foley — one-tap sound design for satisfying video

Shoot ten seconds of anything, place the contact points on a waveform, and get the layered close-mic sound design that makes a clip satisfying — plus the stems, which is what creators actually want.

Why this one
The research proves ASMR is a superb acquisition medium. So sell tools to the people exploiting that, rather than competing with them for retention. It is the only concept whose day-14 driver is external economic incentive — a creator opens it because their posts perform and their account grows, which is a far sturdier motivation than delight. Prosumer subscriptions also convert much better than consumer ones.
The required fix
Drop the automatic material classifier. Detecting the material from a phone clip is the hardest engineering in the whole portfolio and its failure mode is uncanny rather than merely wrong. The value people pay for is the library plus one-tap layering plus stem export — a five-week build instead of twelve, that fails gracefully.
Second fix
Randomise within each material per user. If every clip draws from the same 400 samples, audiences learn to hear "that's the Foley app," the output stops performing, and the only reason anyone opened it disappears.
Honest risks
CapCut sits at #11 with ByteDance behind it and could ship this as a checkbox. Apple's clone rule (4.3(b)) targets exactly this — another video tool in a saturated category. And 20K organic installs a month for a niche creator tool is fantasy; plan on 1–3K.

THE TEST THAT KILLS IT → Hand-produce sound design for 20 creators' clips for free. If their versions with your audio don't measurably outperform their versions without, there is no product.

A3Biggest market, hardest fight

Downshift — a twenty-minute wind-down that ends before you sleep

You hold the phone, tap along with your breathing for twenty seconds, and a haptic pulse locks to your rate then slows by one beat per minute, with a binaural bed phase-locked to it. Then it ends, and you put the phone down.

Why this one
Sleep is the one place the evidence says people genuinely pay. Health & Fitness leads every RevenueCat 2026 category on install value ($1.21 LTV, $0.48 D14 revenue per install, ~6× gaming) and is 68% annual-plan. Consumers demonstrably accept $50–70/year here. Most tellingly, Calm spun sleep out into a standalone app at its own $69.99/yr in September 2025 and raised prices in February 2026 — the category leader validating sleep as a standalone paid product. Sourced
The required fix
It cannot run all night, so stop pretending it will. Core Haptics dies the instant the app backgrounds or the screen locks. Reframe it from an all-night sleep aid to a bounded 20-minute foreground ritual that deliberately ends before sleep onset. Screen-on becomes acceptable, battery and heat stop mattering, and the platform gate stops being existential. Price at $39.99–49.99/yr, not Calm tier.
Honest risks
This is the hardest fight in the portfolio and you should go in knowing it. Sleep is among the most expensive keyword sets on the store — plausible CPI runs $4–9 against roughly $1–2 of gross per install, so you cannot buy a single install profitably. It is organic-only or nothing. BetterSleep or Calm can bolt a haptic taper onto an existing catalogue in six weeks and cross-sell it to millions. And there is a genuine twelve-month problem: one experience, identical on night 200, against a renewal that needs something to have happened.
Unvalidated premise
Be clear that the core bet is a hypothesis, not a finding: a vibrating object in the hand is an arousing stimulus for many people. That it soothes is the assumption the entire product rests on.

THE TEST THAT KILLS IT → Two things in week one, before any other code. First, confirm on-device what happens to haptics when the screen locks. Second, hand fifteen people a rough build and ask whether they'd rather hold the phone or put it on the nightstand. If they put it down, the haptic premise needs a wearable and this is a different product.

09

Compliance tripwires

Verified against Apple's live developer documentation, which was one of only two domains reachable this session. Verified

  • Loot-box odds must be disclosed before purchase (3.1.1). Every concept here avoids randomised purchasables entirely, which sidesteps this and the EU/Brazil loot-box regimes at zero design cost. Keep it that way.
  • Interstitials must be dismissible with large close buttons and in-app ad reporting (2.5.18).
  • Subscriptions need 7-day minimum terms and demonstrable ongoing value; bait-and-switch and gating value behind social posts are explicitly banned (3.1.2). Put "Cancel subscription" at the top level and route it straight to the system sheet.
  • Clone rule 4.3(b), tightened 8 June 2026, blocks new entries in saturated categories — and explicitly names sound effects. Shipping another ASMR sound library is a probable rejection, not just an unoriginal idea.
  • The age system changed. Five tiers went live July 2025; from September 2026 a Social Media capability declaration is mandatory, which pulls an app into iOS 27's parental Time Allowances — a platform-level cap on engagement for Games, Entertainment and Social Media. Shipping in Health & Fitness or Productivity keeps you outside those caps.
  • Age-assurance laws are live, and they bind developers, not just stores. Texas from 4 June 2026, Utah from 6 May, Louisiana from 1 July.
  • Apple's fees are now regional. Baseline 30%, or 15% under the Small Business Program (≤$1M proceeds) — which is you. China cut to 25%/12% in March 2026; Brazil and Japan run an unbundled 21% + 5% structure. Physical goods stay outside IAP entirely, which is what makes A1 work.
10

What I would do first

If you build one thing: Pour. It is the only concept sitting in a mechanic with proven downloads and proven IAP revenue, in the one chart that still lets newcomers in, using the sensory layer where the evidence says it helps rather than where it fails.

If you want the cheapest possible information about whether you can build an audience: Earwitness, as a web page, this month, for about zero dollars.

If you want the most defensible business rather than the biggest one: Thock — and start by brokering ten sales by hand.

Whatever you pick, the sequence matters more than the pick. Every one of these has a two-to-three week test above that costs a fraction of the build and can kill it. Run the test first. The median game that reaches the US top 15 is gone in eight days; the cost of finding that out after four months of building is the thing most likely to end this.

Where this research is weakest

Because fetching was blocked, I could not open a single primary report — no RevenueCat PDF, no GameAnalytics report body, no Sensor Tower release. The chart data in §2 and §3 is first-hand and solid.
