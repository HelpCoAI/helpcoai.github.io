# 05 — Lead Magnets and How to Scale Advertising

## What a lead magnet actually is

A lead magnet is a **complete solution to a narrow problem, given away free, that reveals
a further problem your paid offer solves.**

Every clause matters:

- **Complete** — it must fully solve the problem it addresses. A partial solution creates
  frustration, and frustration doesn't buy.
- **Narrow** — solve one small thing entirely, rather than one big thing partially.
- **Reveals a further problem** — this is the mechanism. Solving the narrow problem
  should naturally surface the next, larger problem, which is what you sell. Not a
  bait-and-switch; a genuine sequence.

The frequent mistake is giving away something *nice* rather than something that creates
demand. A useful checklist PDF that leaves the prospect exactly where they started is a
cost, not an asset.

## Types that work

**1. Reveal a problem (diagnostic).** An audit, assessment, score, or test that shows the
prospect something true and uncomfortable about their situation they didn't previously
know. Highest-converting category, because it manufactures urgency out of reality rather
than out of a deadline. The prospect arrives at the problem themselves, which means they
don't argue with it.

**2. A sample or trial.** A limited taste of the real thing — time-limited,
volume-limited, or feature-limited. Works when the product is genuinely good and the
gap between experiencing it and wanting it is small.

**3. One step of a multi-step process.** Do the first step completely and for free, where
the remaining steps are where the real work is. Naturally sequential; naturally leads to
the sale.

**4. Free with a hitch.** Free but they cover shipping, or free with a commitment, or
free contingent on a call. The small friction filters for intent — which raises lead
quality dramatically even as it lowers lead volume. Usually a good trade.

## The delivery rule

Whatever the magnet is, **the handoff to the paid offer must happen while attention is
still hot**. A magnet consumed in silence, followed by a nurture email three days later,
wastes most of its power. The transition from "here's your free result" to "here's what
we'd do about it" should be immediate and in the same interaction wherever possible.

## Scaling advertising: More, Better, New

Once something works, there are exactly three ways to get more from it, and they go in
this order:

### 1. More

Do more of what's already working before you do anything else. This is the boring answer
and it is almost always the right one. Most businesses abandon a working channel at 10%
of its capacity because they got bored, or because a new tactic looked more interesting.

Ask honestly: *is this channel saturated, or am I just tired of it?* Saturation has
evidence — rising cost per lead, falling response rates at constant quality. Boredom
doesn't.

### 2. Better

Improve conversion at each step of the existing channel. Better hook, better offer,
better follow-up, better sales process, better onboarding. Improvements compound
multiplicatively across the funnel — a 20% lift at three sequential steps is a 73% lift
overall.

Better also has better economics than New: you're improving an asset you already
understand, with data you already have.

### 3. New

Add a new channel. Genuinely the *last* resort, because a new channel resets you to
incompetence — new learning curve, new failure modes, new spend with no benchmarks. It's
also seductive, because early failure in a new channel feels like progress in a way that
grinding an existing one doesn't.

**The discipline:** don't add a new channel until the current one is either saturated or
systematized enough to run without you.

## Doing more without doing it yourself

The escalation past your own capacity:

```
Do it  →  Do it better  →  Document it  →  Delegate it  →  Buy it
```

The step people skip is **document it**. You cannot delegate what you haven't written
down, and the attempt produces the classic failure where a hire underperforms and the
founder concludes "I have to do everything myself" — when in fact they handed over a
process that only existed in their head.

## Applied to HelpCo AI

### The primary lead magnet: the Missed-Call Audit

This is a category-1 diagnostic, and it's close to ideal:

- **Complete** — it fully answers "am I missing calls, and when?"
- **Narrow** — it answers only that.
- **Reveals the next problem** — once they know they're missing calls, the obvious next
  question is *what do I do about it*, which is your paid offer. The sequence is honest;
  you're not manufacturing anything.
- **Nearly free to produce** and highly automatable.
- **Impossible to dispute**, because the evidence is about their own business.

### The existing assets are already lead magnets — treat them as such

The repo has `scorecard.html`, `calculator.html`, and `free-demo.html`. These are
already the right three shapes:

| Asset | Type | Job |
|---|---|---|
| `calculator.html` | Diagnostic | Converts vague worry into a dollar figure using *their* numbers |
| `scorecard.html` | Diagnostic | Scores their current setup, exposes gaps |
| `free-demo.html` | Sample | Lets them hear the AI actually handle a call |

Three things would materially improve them:

1. **The calculator must output a dollar amount, not a grade.** "You're losing roughly
   $4,100/month" creates action. "Your score is 62/100" creates a shrug.
2. **The demo should use their business name.** Hearing the AI answer *"Thanks for calling
   Bayshore Heating & Air"* collapses the imagination gap entirely. This is the single
   highest-value upgrade on the list.
3. **The handoff must be immediate.** The moment the calculator returns a number, the next
   thing on screen is booking a call — not a "check your email" step.

### The magnet-to-offer sequence

```
Cold call rings out  →  send them their own ring-out evidence
                     →  calculator turns it into a monthly dollar figure
                     →  demo lets them hear the fix in their own company's voice
                     →  paid offer, with the audit already done
```

Each step solves one narrow problem completely and reveals the next. By the time the
paid offer appears, the prospect has already established, using their own data, that they
have an expensive problem — and has heard the solution working. That's the whole design.

### On More/Better/New for this business

The temptation, given fifteen industry pages and a dozen service pages, will be to keep
adding surface area. Resist it. The current state of this business is that **no channel
has been pushed anywhere near saturation.**

Concretely: if cold outreach with the ring-out evidence produces customers, the correct
next move is to do that same thing to 500 more businesses — not to add TikTok, not to
build a new service line, not to launch a podcast. More, until it demonstrably stops
working. Then better. Then new.
