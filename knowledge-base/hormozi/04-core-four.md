# 04 — The Core Four (and Lead Getters)

## First, define "lead" correctly

A lead is not a name on a list. A lead is a **person you can contact**. And the only ones
that matter are **engaged leads** — people who have shown interest by doing something:
replying, clicking, booking, opting in, showing up.

This distinction is not pedantry. It redirects effort away from list-building volume and
toward the thing that actually predicts revenue. Ten thousand scraped emails is not ten
thousand leads. Forty people who replied is forty leads.

**Metric that follows from this:** track engaged leads per unit of effort, not
impressions, not reach, not followers.

## The only four ways to advertise

Every advertising activity that has ever existed is one of four things. The taxonomy
comes from crossing two binaries — *who are they* (people who know you vs. strangers) and
*how many at once* (one-to-one vs. one-to-many):

|  | **One-to-one** | **One-to-many** |
|---|---|---|
| **People who know you** | ① Warm outreach | ② Post free content |
| **Strangers** | ③ Cold outreach | ④ Paid ads |

That's it. There is no fifth. Everything you've heard described as a growth channel is
one of these four wearing a costume.

The practical value of the taxonomy: when growth stalls, you're not lost in an infinite
space of tactics. You're either doing one of four things insufficiently, or badly, or
you're neglecting one entirely.

### ① Warm outreach — one-to-one, people who know you

Contacting people who already know you: past customers, dormant customers, your personal
network, anyone who ever inquired.

- **Cheapest and fastest source of first revenue**, and the one almost everyone skips
  because it feels like begging.
- The correct frame is not asking for a favor — it's leading with genuine value.
- **Dormant customer lists are the single most underrated asset in most businesses.**
  People who already bought, already trust you, and already have the problem again.
- Not scalable indefinitely, but it should be exhausted before anything else, and
  revisited perpetually as the list grows.

### ② Post free content — one-to-many, people who know you

Publishing to an audience that follows you.

- **Slowest to start, best long-run economics.** Compounds; costs nothing per unit;
  builds trust at scale.
- The structural model for any piece: **Hook → Retain → Reward.** Get attention, hold it,
  make the time spent worth it. Most content fails at hook; most *good* content fails at
  reward.
- The governing principle: **give away the secret, sell the implementation.** Fear of
  giving away too much is misplaced — knowing *what* to do rarely displaces the demand
  for someone to *do it*. Publishing your actual method builds more trust than withholding
  it, and the people who'd have DIY'd it were never buyers.
- Content also **sifts**: a well-targeted piece attracts the right prospects and repels
  the wrong ones, which lowers downstream sales cost.

### ③ Cold outreach — one-to-one, strangers

Contacting people who don't know you: cold calls, cold email, DMs, door-knocking.

- **Fastest way to get from zero to data.** No audience needed, no ad budget needed.
- Brutal volume-to-response ratios — which is precisely why *personalization and a strong
  first line* matter more than list size.
- The highest-leverage variable is not the script, it's whether your opening demonstrates
  you know something specific and true about *their* business.
- Compliance matters and varies by channel and jurisdiction. Cold calling businesses,
  cold emailing with an opt-out, and outreach to publicly listed business numbers are
  generally workable; automated texting to numbers that haven't opted in is where
  businesses get into real legal trouble in the US. Know the rules for your channel
  before you scale it.

### ④ Paid ads — one-to-many, strangers

Buying attention.

- **The most scalable and the most immediately expensive.** You can turn it up on Monday.
- Works only when the **money model** supports it — you cannot outspend the market on
  acquisition until you know your LTGP:CAC and payback period (see [06](06-money-models.md)).
- Running paid ads before you have a proven offer is how businesses light money on fire.
  Ads amplify whatever is already there, including a bad offer.
- Sequence: prove the offer converts through warm and cold outreach *first*, then buy
  volume against a known conversion rate.

## The order of operations

For a business with little cash and little audience, the sequence is:

```
Warm outreach  →  Cold outreach  →  Content (start immediately, expect slow)  →  Paid ads
```

Warm first because it's free and fast. Cold second because it produces data and revenue
without an audience. Content started in parallel from day one because it takes months to
compound and there is no way to accelerate that except by starting earlier. Paid ads last,
funded by the margin the first three produced, and only once the offer is proven.

**The Rule of 100:** commit to 100 units of advertising work per day, every day —
100 outreach contacts, or 100 minutes of content production, or $100 of ad spend. The
point is less the specific number than the *daily floor that doesn't move*. Advertising
failure is nearly always inconsistency rather than incompetence.

## Lead Getters: getting other people to do it

The Core Four is what *you* do. It has a ceiling — your hours. Past that ceiling, growth
comes from other people doing the advertising for you. Four sources:

**1. Customer referrals.** The cheapest leads that exist, and they convert at rates no
other channel matches because trust transfers with the introduction. Referrals should be
*engineered*, not hoped for: ask at the moment of peak satisfaction (right after a
visible win), make the ask specific rather than general ("who else in your trade
association has this problem?" beats "know anyone?"), and remove all friction from the
introduction.

**2. Employees.** Hiring people to do outreach, content, or sales. Converts your time
constraint into a money constraint, which is the trade you want to make. Requires the
process to be documented well enough to hand over — which means you must do it yourself
first, long enough to know what works.

**3. Agencies.** Buying expertise and execution. Best used to *learn* a channel quickly
and then bring it in-house, rather than as a permanent arrangement. The structural
problem with agencies is that their incentives are attached to spend or retainer, not to
your unit economics.

**4. Affiliates and partners.** Other businesses that already have your customers'
attention and sell them something non-competing. The highest-leverage of the four,
because a single partnership can deliver what months of cold outreach would. Requires
your money model to support paying out a share — which is another reason margin matters.

## Applied to HelpCo AI

### ① Warm outreach — start here, this week

Every business owner you know, every past client, every person from a previous venture,
every chamber and BNI contact. In this market there is also a specific unlock: **local
service businesses are a dense referral graph.** Plumbers know HVAC guys know roofers,
because they subcontract to each other and refer overflow.

Every early customer should be asked, at the moment their dashboard shows the first
recovered job: *"Which other contractor do you actually trust in this area?"*

### ③ Cold outreach — this is your killer channel, and it has a unique mechanic

Most cold outreach opens with a claim. Yours can open with **evidence about their own
business**:

1. Pull the Google Business Profile list of HVAC companies in a target ZIP.
2. Call each one at 7pm on a weekday, and again Saturday morning.
3. Record which ones ring out to voicemail. (In most markets this is a large fraction.)
4. Send that specific business their own recording.

The message writes itself, and it is not a pitch — it's an observation with an artifact
attached:

> "Called you Tuesday 7:12pm and Saturday 9:40am — went to voicemail both times. Not a
> sales thing, just thought you'd want to know, here's the recording. If it's useful, we
> answer those calls for HVAC companies around Sarasota. If not, no worries — keep the
> recording."

This is a **problem-revealing lead magnet delivered as cold outreach**. It's specific,
it's true, it's free to produce, it's impossible to argue with, and it establishes
likelihood before you've made a single claim. This one mechanic is worth more than any
other tactic in this file.

**Compliance note:** these are publicly listed business lines, and you're calling and
following up manually — that's ordinary B2B prospecting. Do check Florida's two-party
consent rules on call recording before you send anyone a recording; the safe version is
to record only your own outbound call ringing out to voicemail (no conversation occurred,
so there's nothing to consent to), or simply to screenshot the call log with timestamps
rather than sending audio. Get this checked by someone qualified before scaling it. Do
not auto-text businesses that haven't engaged.

### ② Content — start now, expect nothing for six months

The hook that fits this business is **local, specific, and verifiable**:

- "We called 50 Sarasota HVAC companies at 7pm. 34 didn't answer. Here's the list of what
  that costs." *(Anonymized — do not name and shame local businesses you intend to sell
  to.)*
- "This plumber missed a $12,000 repipe because he was under a sink. Here's the recording
  of the call he never heard."
- Weekly teardown of one anonymized local business's phone responsiveness.

Publish where these operators actually are: Facebook (local trade groups are extremely
active), YouTube for longer teardowns, and LinkedIn for the professional-services
verticals. Give away the entire method — including how to set up basic missed-call
text-back yourself. The operators who would have built it themselves were never customers;
the ones who read it and think *"I am never doing this"* are.

### ④ Paid ads — not yet

Hold until you have (a) the offer converting from cold outreach, and (b) a measured
LTGP:CAC. Then geo-fenced ads to trade-specific interests, retargeting anyone who used the
calculator or scorecard. The scorecard and calculator pages become the ad destination, not
the homepage.

### Lead Getters — the partnership unlock

The highest-leverage move available to this business is **partnering with the people who
already sell to Gulf Coast contractors**:

- Trade-supply distributors (HVAC and plumbing wholesalers)
- Bookkeepers and CPAs specializing in the trades
- Field service software resellers (ServiceTitan, Housecall Pro, Jobber consultants)
- Trade associations and local contractor groups
- Commercial insurance brokers who write contractor policies

Each of these has a list of exactly your buyer, an existing trust relationship, and no
competing product. A revenue share on a recurring subscription is a genuinely attractive
offer to them — which is another argument for pricing with enough margin to pay it.
