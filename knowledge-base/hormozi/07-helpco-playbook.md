# 07 — The HelpCo Playbook

Everything in files 01–06, assembled into one plan. This file is the deliverable; the
others are the reasoning behind it.

## The one-paragraph strategy

Stop selling "AI automation for local businesses" to fifteen industries. Become **the AI
front desk for Gulf Coast HVAC companies**, sell entry through a paid database
reactivation sprint that pays for itself in week one, upsell the monthly front desk while
their phone is ringing from that reactivation, and acquire customers by calling them at
7pm and sending them the recording of their own phone ringing out.

## The five decisions

### 1. Narrow to one trade

HVAC first. Then plumbing. Then roofing. Keep the other industry pages for SEO; point
every ad, every outbound message, every case study, and the homepage hero at one trade at
a time. Expect this to feel like shrinking the business and to increase revenue anyway.

### 2. Lead with the audit, not the pitch

The Missed-Call Audit is the entire top of funnel. It's free, it costs almost nothing to
run, and it produces evidence about the prospect's own business that no claim can match.
Nothing else you can build has this ratio of cost to persuasive power.

### 3. Put a cash-positive attraction offer in front of the subscription

Database Reactivation Sprint, priced as a one-time engagement with a
books-more-than-it-costs guarantee. This is the change that converts HelpCo from a
cash-constrained subscription business into a self-funding one.

### 4. Sell the subscription as the consequence, not the pitch

Reactivation creates call volume. Call volume creates the problem the AI front desk
solves. The upsell conversation happens when their phone is ringing, and it consists
mostly of pointing at what's already happening.

### 5. Instrument everything before spending on ads

CAC fully loaded, gross margin net of AI and telephony costs, churn by trade, 30-day
gross profit, payback period. No paid acquisition until these are real numbers.

## The 90-day plan

### Days 1–30: Proof

**Goal: 3–5 paying customers in one trade, and one documented case study.**

- Build the target list: every HVAC company in Sarasota, Bradenton, Lakewood Ranch,
  Venice, Parrish. Google Business Profiles plus the Florida contractor license database.
  Expect 150–400 businesses. This list is an asset — maintain it properly.
- Run the audit protocol on the top 100: call at 7pm weekday and 9am Saturday, log which
  ring out, timestamp everything.
- Contact every ring-out with their own evidence. No pitch in the first message.
- Warm outreach in parallel: every business owner you know, every past contact, every
  chamber and BNI connection. Ask for introductions to contractors specifically.
- Run the reactivation sprint for the first customers **manually if necessary**. Do it
  badly and by hand before you automate it. You cannot document a process you haven't
  personally run.
- Fix the calculator to output dollars. Make the demo say the prospect's business name.
- Start publishing. Two pieces a week, minimum, and expect them to do nothing yet.

**Rule of 100 for this phase:** 100 outbound contacts per day. Not a target — a floor.

### Days 31–60: Systematize

**Goal: repeatability, and the numbers that let you scale.**

- Write down the reactivation process step by step, to the level where someone else could
  execute it. This is the step that gets skipped and it's the one that determines whether
  day 61 onward works.
- Build the reporting the guarantee depends on. `dashboard-live.html` needs to show
  recovered calls, booked jobs, and dollars attributable — visible to the customer, not
  just to you. Without this the performance guarantee is unenforceable and will cost you
  a refund argument you can't win.
- Compute the five numbers from [06](06-money-models.md). Honestly. Include your own
  hours in CAC.
- Turn the first customers into case studies with real figures, named businesses, and
  permission. One local named HVAC company with a dollar number outperforms any volume of
  generic proof.
- Ask every customer for referrals at the moment their dashboard shows a recovered job.
  Ask specifically: *"which other contractor in this area do you actually trust?"*
- Open partnership conversations: HVAC/plumbing wholesalers, contractor-focused
  bookkeepers, field service software consultants, trade associations.

### Days 61–90: Scale

**Goal: more of what works, then better, then paid.**

- More: same outreach, 500 more businesses. Extend geographically before extending by
  trade.
- Better: improve the conversion rate at each step using the data from days 1–60. Hook,
  audit-to-call rate, call-to-close rate, onboarding completion, churn.
- Delegate the outreach — this is the first hire, and it's possible only because you
  documented the process in phase two.
- Only now: paid ads, geo-fenced, driving to the calculator and scorecard rather than the
  homepage. Fund them from reactivation cash, and hold them to a payback-period target.
- Begin the second trade — plumbing — by copy-pasting the entire playbook with the nouns
  changed.

## What not to do

Listed because each of these is the thing that will feel most appealing at the moment it
would do the most damage:

- **Don't add service lines.** Thirteen is already too many. Every new one dilutes the
  offer and splits your attention. Sell fewer things to more people.
- **Don't widen back out to fifteen industries** the first time HVAC gets hard. Difficulty
  in a good market is normal and is not a signal to switch markets.
- **Don't run paid ads before the offer converts cold.** Ads amplify whatever exists,
  including a weak offer, and they'll amplify it expensively.
- **Don't discount when you hit price objections.** Diagnose which value-equation term is
  actually weak — it's almost always likelihood — and fix that instead.
- **Don't offer the performance guarantee until the tracking is airtight.**
- **Don't fake scarcity or urgency.** Gulf Coast contractors are a small, talkative
  market. Real constraints only. You have real ones.
- **Don't skip the documentation step** because you're busy selling. It's the difference
  between a business and a job.

## The metrics dashboard

Track weekly. If a number isn't here, it probably doesn't deserve a decision:

| Metric | Why |
|---|---|
| Outbound contacts made | The input you control. Everything downstream is a rate applied to it. |
| Engaged leads (replied, booked, used calculator) | The real lead count |
| Audit → sales call rate | Is the evidence persuasive? |
| Sales call → close rate | Is the offer strong? |
| CAC, fully loaded | Includes your hours |
| 30-day gross profit per customer | The CFA test |
| Payback period | Determines max growth rate |
| LTGP:CAC | Target 3:1 floor; above 10:1 means spend more |
| Monthly churn by trade | Retention is an LTGP lever with no CAC cost |
| Gross margin net of AI/telephony spend | Watch this compress as customers succeed |

## Where the leverage actually is

If you did only three things from this entire knowledge base:

1. **Call 100 HVAC companies at 7pm and send the ones that don't answer their own
   recording.** Costs nothing. No claim you can write competes with it.
2. **Sell the reactivation sprint before the subscription.** Converts the business from
   cash-constrained to self-funding, and manufactures the need for the subscription.
3. **Pick one trade and refuse to widen for two quarters.** Everything else compounds off
   this decision, and it's the one that will be hardest to hold.
