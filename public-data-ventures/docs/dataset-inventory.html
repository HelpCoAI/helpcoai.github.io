PART 1

The dataset inventory

Each entry answers the question that actually determines whether this is a business: is it free, is it fresh, and — most important — who already sells it, and to whom. A dataset with no commercial layer on top of it is raw material. One with three funded competitors is a research citation, not an opening.

A

Business & corporate data

SEC EDGAR — full-text search + XBRL company factsCROWDED
Contains
Every XBRL-tagged financial fact for every US public filer since 2009; full-text search across 20M+ filings back to 2001. JSON.
Access
Free, no key. data.sec.gov/api/xbrl/companyfacts/CIK##########.json; bulk companyfacts.zip. 10 req/sec, requires a descriptive User-Agent header.
License
Fully public domain, unrestricted commercial use.
Monetized by
sec-api.io, Intrinio, AlphaSense, Bamboo, EDGAR Online (acquired, historic) already repackage this at $50–500/mo.
Gotchas
Company-specific "extension" XBRL tags break cross-company comparability; pre-2009 filings have no XBRL at all; full-text search needs real NLP to be useful beyond keyword matching.
USPTO PatentsView + Trademark bulk data (TSDR)PARTIAL
Contains
Every US patent grant/application with inventor, assignee, citation, and classification data since 1976; full trademark case history and prosecution documents.
Access
Free. PatentsView API needs no key; TSDR bulk data requires a free API key (developer.uspto.gov).
License
Public domain.
Monetized by
Clarivate/IFI Claims, PatSnap, Anaqua at $10k+/yr enterprise contracts sold to Fortune 500 IP departments.
Gotchas
Assignee name disambiguation is the field's classic hard problem — subsidiaries, spelling variants, and M&A renaming all break naive joins. This is the actual moat, not the data itself.
SAM.gov entity registrations + exclusionsPARTIAL
Contains
~2M active federal-vendor entity registrations: UEI, NAICS, POC, size status, exclusions/debarments.
Access
Free API key required (rate-limited). Entity Extracts API returns up to 1M records/call as CSV/JSON. open.gsa.gov/api/entity-api.
License
Public, FOIA-releasable extract explicitly designed for reuse.
Monetized by
GovTribe, HigherGov, Deltek GovWin — $100s–$1,000s/mo aimed at large primes.
Gotchas
2022's DUNS→UEI migration broke joins with every pre-2022 dataset; NAICS codes are self-reported and frequently stale; registration lapses (entities that let SAM registration expire) are an underused churn signal.
State business registries (Secretary of State filings, 50 states)CROWDED
Contains
LLC/corp formation, officers, registered agents, status. No federal registry exists — fully fragmented by state.
Access
Varies wildly. CO, OR, CT, IA publish real open bulk data; most states charge for bulk or offer search-only.
License
Public record, but several states license bulk resale for a fee.
Monetized by
OpenCorporates, Middesk, Cobalt Intelligence, Bizapedia, Dun & Bradstreet, LexisNexis at $300–500+/mo. Already saturated at national scope.
Gotchas
No canonical cross-state entity ID; dissolved/inactive status lags by months; this category is genuinely crowded — skip it as a primary bet.
FCC ULS (Universal Licensing System)GREENFIELD
Contains
Every wireless, broadcast, and spectrum license: callsign, licensee, frequency, coordinates, expiration.
Access
Free, no key. Daily pipe-delimited bulk downloads plus weekly full-database dump. fcc.gov/wireless/data/public-access-files-database-downloads.
License
Fully public, no restriction.
Monetized by
Only small scraper-repackagers on marketplaces like Apify — no dominant player at the tower-leasing/spectrum-broker layer.
Gotchas
Linking a callsign/FRN to the real corporate owner behind a shell licensee requires cross-referencing SEC/state filings — real but tractable work.
B

Property & geospatial

County property assessor & deed recordsCROWDED
Contains
Parcel ownership, assessed value, sales history, deed transfers — 3,000+ county jurisdictions, no federal aggregator.
Access
County-by-county; formats range from clean GIS shapefile to PDF-only, some in-person only.
License
Public record, terms vary by county.
Monetized by
ATTOM (160M properties), CoreLogic (152M), Regrid (150M parcels), Black Knight — mature, enterprise-licensed at $10k–$1M+/yr.
Gotchas
National-scope parcel data is a solved, saturated market. The remaining opening is narrow verticals — code-violation-to-assessor joins, specific under-covered rural counties — not "build another nationwide parcel database."
FEMA National Flood Hazard LayerPARTIAL
Contains
Effective flood zone geometry covering >90% of the US population, continuously revised.
Access
Free ArcGIS REST API + full geodatabase bulk download. fema.gov/flood-maps/national-flood-hazard-layer.
License
Public domain.
Monetized by
National Flood Data, LightBox, First American sell parcel-level flood-zone lookups to insurers and mortgage lenders.
Gotchas
Map panels are different vintages county to county (some decades old); preliminary vs. effective maps disagree; address→parcel→flood-zone matching is the actual hard part.
EPA ECHO (Enforcement & Compliance History Online)GREENFIELD
Contains
1.5M+ regulated facilities, 130+ fields: inspections, violations, penalties under Clean Air/Water/RCRA/SDWA.
Access
Free, no key. ECHO Exporter full bulk ZIP updated weekly, plus REST web services. echo.epa.gov/tools/data-downloads.
License
Fully public.
Monetized by
Thin — a few ESG-adjacent research shops, no dominant mid-market player despite rising supply-chain-diligence demand.
Gotchas
No clean facility→parent-company linkage (EPA's Facility Registry Service doesn't map to corporate ownership) — that linkage is the moat.
National Address Database (NAD) + NOAA/USGS reference layersPARTIAL
Contains
~80M address points contributed voluntarily by state/local GIS offices; NOAA climate/storm archives; USGS elevation/LiDAR and water-gauge data.
Access
Free bulk download, DOT-maintained. transportation.gov/gis/national-address-database. NOAA via api.weather.gov + NCEI bulk archives.
License
Public domain — but coverage is voluntary and patchy (whole regions of some states are missing).
Monetized by
Smarty, Melissa Data, Loqate dominate proprietary address verification — but they don't rely on NAD; they build their own. NAD's specific gaps remain unaddressed.
Gotchas
The real USPS address database is not public — it's licensed only through CASS-certified vendors. NAD is the free-but-incomplete alternative; filling its gaps is the opportunity, not reselling it as-is.
C

Health

CMS Open Payments ("Sunshine Act")PARTIAL
Contains
15M+ general-payment records/yr + 750k+ research-payment records: every pharma/device dollar to a covered physician, back to 2013.
Access
Free, no key. Full bulk CSV + REST API. openpaymentsdata.cms.gov.
License
No restrictions on the underlying data.
Monetized by
ProPublica's free "Dollars for Docs" covers the nonprofit layer; commercially, Definitive Healthcare and IQVIA-style vendors sell KOL mapping to pharma HQ. Regional/mid-size med-affairs teams remain underserved by enterprise pricing.
Gotchas
Physician name-matching to NPI requires fuzzy joins; payment categorization taxonomy changes across publication years.
NPPES / NPI RegistryPARTIAL
Contains
8M+ National Provider Identifiers: name, taxonomy, address, deactivation status.
Access
Free. Weekly/monthly bulk ZIP (V2 format as of March 2026) + real-time lookup API.
License
Public domain.
Monetized by
Definitive Healthcare, HealthLink Dimensions and various "provider-data-as-a-service" vendors sell enriched feeds.
Gotchas
Raw NPPES has no Medicare enrollment (PECOS), exclusion (LEIE), or license-board status — enrichment across those four sources is where the value actually sits.
Hospital price-transparency machine-readable filesCROWDED
Contains
Legally mandated gross charges, payer-negotiated rates, and cash prices for ~6,000 hospitals plus payer MRFs.
Access
Free — posted by each hospital/payer on their own site, no central bulk source. Must be crawled.
License
Public by federal mandate.
Monetized by
Turquoise Health ($40M Series C, March 2026), Serif Health — this category is now well-funded and actively competitive at the platform level.
Gotchas
Arguably the messiest dataset on this list: generic "Payer 1/Payer 2" labels, non-standard schemas, and files that don't match their own hospital's chargemaster. Turquoise reported a 96.6% de-duplication rate cleaning a single payer's files — that gives you a sense of the mess.
openFDA (recalls, adverse events, device/drug labeling)GREENFIELD
Contains
MAUDE device adverse events, FAERS drug adverse events, all recall classes, structured product labeling.
Access
Free, Elasticsearch-backed REST API, no key required for low volume. open.fda.gov/apis. Updates ~weekly.
License
Explicitly built for reuse, commercial use allowed.
Monetized by
No dominant commercial platform — genuinely underexploited relative to its size and the legal/compliance demand around it.
Gotchas
Reports are voluntary/manufacturer-submitted and often duplicated or delayed; raw text fields need real NLP to extract a clean "what happened."
CMS provider utilization PUFs + PECOS enrollmentPARTIAL
Contains
Annual Medicare Part B/D utilization and payment by provider/procedure; PECOS enrollment, opt-out, and ordering/referring authority.
Access
Utilization PUFs: free annual bulk CSV. PECOS itself has no public API — status must be reconstructed via the NPI Registry API's PECOS flags or FOIA.
License
Public.
Monetized by
Bundled into $50k+/yr enterprise platforms (Definitive Healthcare-class vendors); no low-cost, narrow-vertical option exists.
Gotchas
The PECOS API gap is precisely the kind of "annoying to assemble" problem that creates a real moat for a small, patient team.
D

Labor & economic

BLS (CPI, employment, OEWS wages)PARTIAL
Contains
Monthly employment, CPI by region/category, occupational wage estimates by metro and industry.
Access
Free API (registration recommended for higher rate limit) + flat-file bulk. bls.gov/developers.
License
Public domain.
Monetized by
Payscale-class comp-benchmarking vendors at the aggregate level; hyper-local/niche-occupation cuts remain underserved.
Gotchas
Small-area estimates get suppressed for statistical reliability; methodology changes across years complicate long time series.
Census ACS + County Business Patterns + Building Permits SurveyCROWDED
Contains
Demographic/economic estimates (ACS), establishment counts by NAICS/county (CBP), and monthly/annual permit activity by jurisdiction (BPS).
Access
Free API (needs a free key) + bulk CSV. api.census.gov/data.html.
License
Public domain.
Monetized by
ESRI Business Analyst wraps this into enterprise site-selection tooling; Placer.ai and demographic resellers sit on top too.
Gotchas
CBP suppresses small county/industry cells for disclosure avoidance; BPS coverage has gaps in permit offices that don't report.
USASpending.gov / FPDS federal contracts & grantsPARTIAL
Contains
Every federal contract, grant, and loan award — recipient, amount, agency, NAICS/CFDA, place of performance.
Access
Free, no key. api.usaspending.gov plus a full bulk Award Data Archive.
License
Public domain.
Monetized by
GovTribe, HigherGov, Vistant, Deltek GovWin — a proven, growing market priced for large primes ($1,000s/mo).
Gotchas
Recipient entity resolution across the 2022 UEI/DUNS transition; subcontract data (FSRS) is far less complete than prime-award data.
DOL/OSHA enforcement + Wage & Hour Division violationsGREENFIELD
Contains
4M+ OSHA inspection/violation/penalty records since 1973; WHD wage-theft case outcomes.
Access
Free bulk CSV (enforcedata.dol.gov, daily updates) plus a newer free-key API at dataportal.dol.gov.
License
Public domain.
Monetized by
Almost nobody at the dedicated-product level — a genuinely thin commercial layer given how directly it maps to insurance underwriting.
Gotchas
Establishment name/address needs fuzzy matching to the actual insured legal entity — employers file under DBAs, subsidiaries, and job-site addresses inconsistently.
E

Legal & regulatory

PACER/RECAP via CourtListener (Free Law Project)PARTIAL
Contains
Federal docket metadata and a growing share of the actual documents behind PACER's paywall — PACER generates ~$150M/yr charging $0.10/page; RECAP gives away whatever anyone has already paid for.
Access
Free REST API v4. courtlistener.com / free.law/recap.
License
Nonprofit-operated, built explicitly for reuse and downstream products.
Monetized by
Free Law Project itself is a nonprofit, but the category it feeds — Lex Machina (LexisNexis), Docket Alarm (Fastcase), Trellis for state courts — is a proven $100M+ business category.
Gotchas
RECAP coverage is inherently incomplete — only documents someone already paid for and shared exist for free; true completeness still costs PACER fees.
State court records (no RECAP equivalent)CROWDED
Contains
State civil/criminal dockets — fragmented per state/county, most with no bulk export at all.
Access
Scraping required in most states; a handful (e.g. some state judiciary open-data portals) offer real bulk files.
License
Public record, but access mechanics vary and some states restrict bulk scraping in their terms.
Monetized by
Trellis, UniCourt already cover the big states (CA, TX, FL) well. Smaller/rural counties remain genuinely open.
Gotchas
Court-to-court schema drift is severe; entity resolution of party names across cases is a real, ongoing project, not a one-time script.
Federal Register + Regulations.govGREENFIELD
Contains
Every proposed/final rule, notice, and public comment across all federal agencies, daily.
Access
Free APIs, no key for Federal Register; free key for regulations.gov. federalregister.gov/developers/documentation/api/v1.
License
Public domain.
Monetized by
Thin outside enterprise regulatory-intelligence suites (Thomson Reuters) — vertical-specific alerting is largely unbuilt.
Gotchas
Volume is enormous and mostly noise for any one company; the value is entirely in filtering + summarization, which favors an AI-native small team.
State lobbying & campaign financeRESTRICTED
Contains
State-level donations, lobbying registrations and spend. Federal-level (FEC) is clean and free.
Access
FEC: free, api.open.fec.gov. State-level: FollowTheMoney/OpenSecrets bulk data, or individual state portals of wildly varying quality.
License
Not unrestricted — FollowTheMoney/OpenSecrets bulk data is free for non-commercial/research/journalism use; commercial reuse requires separate licensing. Several states have no machine-readable export at all.
Monetized by
OpenSecrets and FollowTheMoney are themselves nonprofit-run; no major commercial reseller at state-lobbying granularity.
Gotchas
Check licensing before building a commercial product directly on FollowTheMoney/OpenSecrets bulk exports — the free tier is explicitly framed for non-commercial use.

Transport & infrastructure

FAA Aircraft RegistryPARTIAL
Contains
Every US-registered civil aircraft: N-number, owner, make/model, airworthiness, address.
Access
Free daily bulk flat file (~60MB CSV/TXT), refreshed nightly. No official API, but trivial to wrap one. faa.gov/licenses_certificates/aircraft_certification/aircraft_registry/releasable_aircraft_download.
License
Public domain.
Monetized by
FlightAware, JetNet, AMSTAT own business-aviation valuation/ownership intelligence at enterprise pricing; small/experimental-aircraft owner marketing lists remain underexploited.
Gotchas
Ownership is frequently held in trusts/LLCs specifically to obscure the beneficial owner — real entity resolution problem for anyone doing outreach.
FMCSA SAFER / motor carrier censusCROWDED
Contains
~2M trucking carriers/brokers/forwarders: DOT number, safety rating, insurance on file, power units.
Access
Free. SAFER Company Snapshot + QCMobile API, no key for basic queries; full census bulk file available.
License
Public, explicitly built to "encourage development of products, tools, or services."
Monetized by
DAT, Highway, Carrier411, MyCarrierPortal — a proven, crowded category among large freight brokerages.
Gotchas
MCS-150 filings only happen every two years, so the official data is often badly stale — the freshness gap between filing and reality is the wedge, since incumbents don't fully close it either.
NHTSA recalls, complaints, VIN decoder, FARS crash dataPARTIAL
Contains
Every safety recall/complaint by VIN/make/model, plus fatal-crash records (FARS).
Access
Free API, no key. nhtsa.gov/nhtsa-datasets-and-apis.
License
Public domain.
Monetized by
CARFAX/AutoCheck dominate vehicle-history but rely on separate proprietary sources (insurance, repair shops) — recall-compliance monitoring specifically for fleets/dealers is a smaller, less contested niche.
Gotchas
Complaint text is free-form and needs NLP triage to separate signal from noise.

Free API, generous rate limits. quickstats.nass.usda.gov/api.

Public domain.

Nonprofit & philanthropic — the proven comp

IRS Business Master File + e-file 990 XML (GivingTuesday S3 Data Lake)PARTIAL
Contains
1.97M nonprofit entities (BMF) + full e-filed 990/990-EZ/990-PF XML, ~324MB compact, back to 2011.
Access
Free, no key. S3 bucket gt990datalake-rawdata (us-east-1), accessible via AWS console or CLI. 990data.givingtuesday.org.
License
Public domain (IRS-published under FOIA).
Monetized by
Candid/Foundation Directory ($35–127/mo), Instrumentl ($179–499/mo), GrantStation ($219/qtr) — all priced and curated for well-known, larger foundations, which structurally excludes small local funders. This is exactly the gap the seed business exploits.
Gotchas
990 filings lag actual fiscal years by 12–18 months on average; small-org XML often has inconsistent schema versions; entity resolution across a funder's name variants is nontrivial at scale.
PART 2

Eighteen businesses

Biased toward: buyer is a business, not a consumer; the dataset is messy enough that cleaning it is the product; freshness or entity resolution is the hard part; and the need repeats monthly rather than satisfying one-time curiosity.

Note on the seed pattern. The team's proven business — filter the IRS BMF geographically to surface small local funders Candid/Instrumentl miss — is not re-listed as idea #1 below since it already exists. Idea #2 is the most direct next step built on the exact same pipeline.

Top 5, ranked

Ranked by willingness-to-pay (is there an existing budget line this replaces or beats?) crossed with feasibility for a 1–3 person AI-capable team in the next two quarters.

#1 Local Funder Warm-Intro Engine

Dataset: IRS BMF + 990 XML (existing pipeline) — board/officer name graph
Buyer
Development directors and grant writers at small nonprofits (<$2M budget) already paying for the scholarship/grant-finder tool.
Why they pay
A list of funders is table stakes; a warm introduction path — "your board chair's spouse sits on this foundation's board" — is the thing that actually moves a cold application into a funded one.
Price
$49–99/mo add-on tier to the existing product; undercuts Instrumentl's $179–499/mo outright.
Moat
Officer/director name-matching across 990 filings is a real NLP dedup problem (name variants, common names, title changes) — not a database lookup.
Why it's open
Candid and Instrumentl don't build relationship graphs; they're search-and-filter tools by design, priced for large-org research teams, not warm-intro workflows.
Build: Low — extends existing pipelineTeam: 1–2Time to revenue: 4–8 weeks

#2 Recall & Adverse-Event Intake Radar

Dataset: openFDA (MAUDE, FAERS, recalls)
Buyer
Plaintiff-side mass-tort/product-liability law firms doing case intake; hospital supply-chain/risk managers.
Why they pay
Mass-tort firms already spend heavily on lead generation; being first to flag a new device-recall pattern with real case volume is directly worth client acquisition dollars to them.
Price
$299–799/mo per practice area tracked, or a per-qualified-lead fee for the legal-intake variant.
Moat
openFDA's raw text is legalese and duplicated; an LLM-native summarizer that turns it into "severity, affected population, likely claim value" is squarely an AI-engineering moat, not a data moat.
Why it's open
No dominant commercial platform exists at this exact intersection — enterprise-side regulatory trackers exist, but nothing purpose-built for legal intake teams.
Build: Low-mediumTeam: 1–2Time to revenue: 6–10 weeks

#3 OSHA/WHD Underwriting Feed

Dataset: DOL OSHA enforcement + Wage & Hour Division violations
Buyer
Workers' comp and general-liability MGAs (managing general agents) and construction surety underwriters — the small/mid-size ones, not the carriers with in-house LexisNexis Risk feeds.
Why they pay
A prospective insured's OSHA violation history directly prices risk; small MGAs currently either skip this check entirely or do it by hand per submission.
Price
$0.50–2/lookup API, or $500–2,000/mo flat feed for higher-volume underwriters.
Moat
Fuzzy-matching an establishment's OSHA record to the actual named-insured legal entity (DBAs, job-site addresses, subsidiary structures) — genuinely hard, daily-refreshed.
Why it's open
Verisk/LexisNexis Risk bundle this into platforms priced for large carriers; nothing purpose-built and affordable exists for small MGAs.
Build: MediumTeam: 2–3Time to revenue: 3–4 months

#4 Credentialing Cross-Check (PECOS + NPI + License Boards)

Dataset: NPPES/NPI Registry + reconstructed CMS PECOS status + state license boards
Buyer
Locum tenens/travel-nurse staffing agencies and hospital medical-staff/credentialing offices doing primary-source verification.
Why they pay
Every day a provider isn't credentialed is a day of lost billing for a hospital and lost placement fee for a staffing agency — this is an urgent, revenue-linked pain point, not a nice-to-have.
Price
$2–5 per provider verified, or $500+/mo subscription for agencies verifying in volume.
Moat
CMS PECOS has no public API — status must be reconstructed by cross-referencing NPI Registry PECOS flags against enrollment records. That reconstruction work is the entire defensibility.
Why it's open
Enterprise CVO (credential-verification-organization) vendors exist but are priced and built for large health systems, not staffing agencies or small clinics.
Build: Medium-highTeam: 2–3Time to revenue: 4–6 months

#5 Vertical Regulatory Alert Line

Dataset: Federal Register + Regulations.gov, one vertical at a time
Buyer
Compliance officers at small/mid companies in one specific regulated vertical (start with cannabis, PFAS/chemicals, or BSA-AML fintech).
Why they pay
They currently either miss relevant rulemakings entirely or pay for Thomson Reuters Regulatory Intelligence at enterprise rates far beyond what a 20-person company can justify.
Price
$199–499/mo per vertical.
Moat
Vertical-specific taxonomy and LLM-quality summarization of dense rulemaking text into "does this affect you, and what do you need to do" — an AI-engineering moat that compounds as a reusable pipeline.
Why it's open
The category is real (compliance officers already buy this) but nobody has built the narrow, cheap, vertical-specific version — and this idea is a product line: the same pipeline repeats across verticals.
Build: Low-mediumTeam: 1–2Time to revenue: 6–8 weeks per vertical

Dataset: State DMV title/salvage/lien records
Buyer
Would-be car buyers, resellers.
Flag: not actually free. State DMV records look like public government data but are federally restricted from resale under the Driver's Privacy Protection Act (DPPA) except to permissible-use holders — insurers, employers doing background checks, not general marketers or consumer apps. This is the exact trap the prompt warns about: government-adjacent data that appears open but isn't. Confirm DPPA permissible-use status before building anything on DMV records.
