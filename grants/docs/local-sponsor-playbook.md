# Local Sponsor Playbook

How to sell local sponsored scholarships — the revenue line that funds the operation
*and* produces the SEO pages that acquire users.

## The pitch is not "buy an ad"

It is: **"Fund a scholarship for local kids. We run the whole thing for you."**

That reframing matters. Local businesses are pitched advertising constantly and have
learned to say no. Almost none of them are pitched a turnkey scholarship program, and
many of them already want one.

## Why the administrative burden is the whole opportunity

Most local businesses that would happily fund a $1,000 scholarship never start one,
because doing it properly means: legal setup, publicizing it, collecting applications,
screening for eligibility, selecting a winner defensibly, handling the payout to the
school, and tax handling. That is weeks of work nobody has.

You already have all of it built — the data, the matching, the applicant pool, the
deadline tracking. Removing that burden *is* the product.

## Who buys

Ranked by likelihood, based on who already sponsors local causes:

| Segment | Why they say yes |
|---|---|
| **Credit unions & community banks** | Achieva and Suncoast already run scholarships. Community reinvestment is a stated priority. Warmest possible lead. |
| **Real estate brokerages & agents** | Heavy local-brand spenders; families are their exact customer |
| **Car dealerships** | Large local marketing budgets, already sponsor youth sports |
| **Law firms** (personal injury, family, estate) | Community goodwill is a direct marketing channel |
| **Medical & dental practices** | Local reputation-driven, often want a giving vehicle |
| **HVAC / plumbing / roofing / home services** | Already sponsor little league; same budget line |
| **Chambers of commerce** | Can sponsor directly or introduce members — a multiplier |
| **Local employers with 50+ staff** | Employee-dependent scholarships double as a retention benefit |

## Pricing

**$2,500–5,000 per sponsorship.** Structure it as $1,000–2,000 to the actual award and
the remainder as your administration fee. Say that split out loud — sponsors respond
well to knowing exactly how much reaches a student.

Twenty sponsors at $2,500 = **$50,000/year**, twenty new scholarships that did not exist
before, and twenty pages that rank in local search.

## What the sponsor gets

- A named scholarship ("The [Business] Community Scholarship")
- Their logo and name on the scholarship page and in the winner announcement
- A photo and story with the recipient — genuinely good local PR
- Applicant volume and demographics, in aggregate (never individual student data)
- Likely tax deduction (confirm treatment with an accountant; this is stronger if the
  program sits inside a 501(c)(3) — see `nonprofit-structure-assessment.md`)

## What the sponsor never gets

**Individual student contact information.** This is the line that protects the entire
business. The moment sponsors get student data, you become a lead-generation company
and the trust evaporates — that is precisely the path Fastweb took. Aggregate reporting
only, and say so in the sponsor agreement.

## How to sell it

**This is a relationship sale, not cold outreach.** Start with businesses you or your
network already know. One warm introduction is worth fifty cold emails.

1. **Start with your own network.** Anyone who already knows you converts far faster.
2. **Lead with the local proof.** "There are 47 scholarship programs in Sarasota and
   Manatee counties. None of them appear on Fastweb or Scholarships.com. Families here
   are missing money that already exists." That opener is true, specific, and nobody
   else can say it.
3. **Bring a recipient story once you have one.** After the first cycle, a photo of a
   local student holding a check sells the next ten sponsorships by itself.
4. **Time it to their budget year, not yours.** Most small businesses set marketing
   budgets in Q4 for the following year.
5. **Anchor to what they already spend.** A little league banner is $500–2,000 and
   reaches a few hundred people for one season. This reaches every college-bound family
   in the county and produces a story worth posting.

## Sequencing

Sponsors are easier to sell *after* you have users, and users are easier to get *after*
you have sponsored scholarships to offer. Break the loop by selling the first two or
three sponsorships on the strength of the data alone — the 47 verified local awards in
`data/03_verified_scholarships.csv` are a credible artifact before a single user signs up.

## On advertising alongside this

Sponsorships and display advertising are different businesses. Sponsorships are aligned:
the sponsor's money becomes a scholarship. Programmatic ads are not: the network serves
whoever bids highest, and in this vertical the highest bidders are student lenders, loan
refinancers, and for-profit colleges.

If you run display ads at all, run them only on public content pages, never inside the
logged-in product, and maintain a hard blocklist on lenders and for-profit colleges.
That costs roughly half the RPM and is worth every dollar.
