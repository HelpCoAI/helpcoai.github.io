Research Reports Economic Policy Making in the Age of Artificial Intelligence This report examines how artificial intelligence is reshaping economic policymaking in an increasingly digital and data-driven world. Drawing on economic theory and international experience, it explores how AI can strengthen governments&#39; ability to achieve sustained economic growth, price stability, full employment, equitable income distribution, poverty reduction, and sound fiscal and external balances. The report analyzes the application of AI to monetary, fiscal, employment, income, international trade, and social policies while addressing the broader institutional challenges of governance, transparency, accountability, privacy, and ethics. Throughout, it combines conceptual analysis with case studies from advanced and developing economies to illustrate both the opportunities and the risks associated with AI-driven policymaking. Rather than viewing AI as a substitute for economic judgment, the report presents it as a powerful tool whose effectiveness ultimately depends on sound institutions, reliable data, and effective governance. ISBN: 978-1-7337604-7-8 Download Full Report .PDF Technology and Human Development For most of human history, economic growth was very slow. Before 1750, it took 6,000 years for global per capita income to double. Since then, it has doubled every 50 years, primarily due to technological advancements. These advancements have significantly boosted per capita incomes and altered social, economic, and political landscapes, though the benefits were not evenly distributed at first. Technological progress enhances productivity, leading to higher incomes, better education, and healthier lives, which in turn foster more innovation and further technological advancements. The Human Development Index (HDI) measures development by evaluating health, education, and living standards, showing strong correlations between productivity, economic growth, and HDI components. Rapid advancements in digital technologies, such as AI, 5G, and cloud computing, are transforming various sectors but also highlight the digital divide, which exacerbates income and wealth inequalities. Mobile phone and internet usage have surged, but access remains uneven, particularly affecting low-income countries and marginalized groups like women and older adults. The cost and quality of internet services also vary significantly, with fixed broadband being much more expensive than mobile broadband in low-income countries. Frontier technologies, such as AI and robotics, present both opportunities and challenges, potentially displacing jobs but also creating high-value positions. Robust education and training programs are crucial to help workers adapt to the evolving job market and reduce income inequality. ISBN: 978-1-7337604-8-5 Download Full Report .PDF Global Inflation Globalization led to the integration of more economies and more labor sources into the global economy. The availability of cheap labor helped accelerate economic growth as well as dampening inflationary pressures. During the last three decades global inflation was very low. With COVID-19 pandemic things started to change. Prices dropped sharply during the first months of the pandemic. Then prices surged at a global level. The pandemic caused further disruptions in economic activity and created supply chain issues, leading to higher commodity and consumer prices. Moreover, governments trying to cope with the pandemic had significant increases in government expenditures and money supply. The average inflation rate reached the Great Recession levels in the middle months of 2020, while the median rate reached that level in October 2022. This study, after a brief introduction to theories of inflation, examines measurement issues, commodity prices and inflation relationship, supply chain issues, disequilibrium in labor markets, government expenditures, and monetary policy using country level data as well as global data where available. ISBN: 978-1-7337604-5-4 Download Full Report .PDF Industrial Policy in Emerging Markets Industrial policy has been a controversial issue among economists. While some economists consider the industrial policies instrumental in the industrialization of recently industrialized economies, the others think the outcomes would be better if these polices were not implemented. The attitudes towards industrial policy have undergone changes over the years. Until 1980s industrial policy was theoretically justified and implemented in many developing and advanced economies. With the Washington Consensus industrial policy has lost its attraction. However, in recent years particularly after the Great Recession, and now with Covid-19 pandemic the role of state in economic policy is debated and industrial policy is becoming an essential part of economic policy in many countries. This study examines the experiences of some selected emerging economies in designing and implementing industrial polices and outcomes they arrived at. The evaluation is conducted in two stages. The first stage is based on the trend analysis of a number of economic indicators such as GDP in the eight economies selected. In the second stage industrial policies are evaluated in more detail in South Korea, Brazil, and Vietnam. The relationship between the policies and economic performance indicators in the distinct policy periods are explored empirically.
